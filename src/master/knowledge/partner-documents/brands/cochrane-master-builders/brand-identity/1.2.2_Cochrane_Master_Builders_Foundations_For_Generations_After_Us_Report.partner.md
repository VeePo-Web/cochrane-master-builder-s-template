## 2. What this document is

The **tagline + mission-statement source of truth**. It articulates "Foundations For Generations After Us" as the central brand promise and provides the surrounding language: candidate taglines, sub-tagline rhythms, hero promise patterns, and the verbal cadence that signals legacy without slipping into cliché.

## 3. Decision triggers — when to consult this doc

- Writing or revising a hero headline, sub-headline, or hero CTA.
- Generating tagline candidates for a new trade remix.
- Mission statement copy on About, Founder, or footer.
- "Promise to the next generation" framing — anywhere the legacy theme is explicit.
- Choosing power words for `src/config/brand-identity-northstar.ts` and per-trade overrides.
- Any copy that needs to feel like it could outlive the people who wrote it.

## 4. Decision types this doc DOES NOT govern

- Tone / voice arrays at the system level → `brand-identity/1.2.1.partner.md` (the Family Legacy Standard).
- Persona-specific empathy → `personas-icp/1.4.x.partner.md`.
- SEO keyword + meta structure → `seo-research/1.1.partner.md` (then wrapped in the voice this doc defines).
- Layout / hero composition → `ux-design/1.3.1.partner.md`.

## 5. How to read it

- Parse PDF first via `document--parse_document`.
- Start with the central tagline + the rationale for "Foundations For Generations After Us."
- Then jump to the candidate alternates — useful for trade-specific remixes that need a sister tagline.
- Finish with the cadence / rhythm rules — these prevent the copy from collapsing into greeting-card territory.

## 6. Routing precedence

- **Wins over `1.2.1`** on tagline phrasing, mission-statement wording, and hero promise patterns.
- **Loses to `1.2.1`** on the *register* (tone words, palette, type philosophy) that wraps those taglines.
- Wins over personas on the master phrasing; personas adapt the cadence per audience.
- Loses to `seo-research/1.1` on the keyword skeleton inside SEO-critical headlines (then this doc dictates how that skeleton is voiced).
